Predicting S&P 500 excess returns and sizing a daily allocation under a volatility constraint

Technical report — PMLDL 2026 project, Stage 2 (Project implementation)

Summary

We study Hull Tactical Market Prediction: each trading day a model outputs an S&P 500 allocation in [0, 2], scored by volatility- and shortfall-penalised modified Sharpe. We reproduce three public baselines, evaluate a hybrid LightGBM with domain indicators, and extend it with a LightGBM/CatBoost/Ridge ensemble and allocation selection. The revised study nests selection inside chronological training periods and compares every evaluated strategy with passive w=1. The label improved names a pipeline stage; it does not establish a statistically reliable performance gain.

1. Problem statement

1.1 Task

For every trading day t the model receives anonymised market features and must return a weight wₜ ∈ [0, 2] for holding the S&P 500 (0 = everything at the risk-free rate, 1 = fully invested, 2 = 2× leverage financed at the risk-free rate). The daily strategy return is rₜ = wₜ · forward_returnₜ + (1 − wₜ) · risk_free_rateₜ. The task therefore has two parts: a forecasting part (predict the next-day excess return) and a decision part (turn the forecast into a position that keeps volatility close to the market's). The competition frames this as a test of the Efficient Market Hypothesis: a strategy should outperform the index without taking more than 120% of its volatility.

The last 180 date_ids are excluded from model fitting and form the public diagnostic. They are not an untouched test: the historical score of 0.011 influenced development (§4.5). No new prospective test data are available; the forecasting phase is out of scope.

1.2 Data source and preprocessing

Data: train.csv from the competition page (https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hull-tactical-market-prediction/data). It has 9,048 rows (date_id 0–9047) and 94 anonymised daily features grouped by prefix: M (market dynamics), E (macro), I (interest rates), P (price/valuation), V (volatility), S (sentiment), D (binary dummies). Three columns describe the future relative to their row and are used only as labels or for scoring: forward_returns, risk_free_rate and the target market_forward_excess_returns (forward return minus its 5-year rolling mean, MAD-winsorised by the organisers).

1.3 Evaluation metrics

Primary metric — modified Sharpe (src/metrics.py: modified_sharpe), a re-implementation of the organisers' public scoring function (https://www.kaggle.com/code/metric/hull-competition-sharpe). Let s be the strategy returns and m the market returns on the scored rows:

Sharpe = geometric-mean daily excess return of s / std(s) × √252;

vol_penalty = 1 + max(0, std(s)/std(m) − 1.2) — volatility up to 120% of the market is free;

return_penalty = 1 + gap² / 100, where gap is the annualised shortfall (in %) of the strategy's geometric mean excess return below the market's;

modified Sharpe = Sharpe / (vol_penalty × return_penalty). Higher is better.

The implementation follows the official formula (divisor penalties, geometric mean, std of raw returns). Unlike the official code it does not raise on degenerate inputs (zero variance or weights outside [0, 2]); it returns a very low score instead, so that searches do not crash. No reported model has weights outside [0, 2] (bounds_violation = 0 for all rows).

Metric | Definition | Better | Why reported
Spearman IC | Rank correlation of prediction and target | higher | Main model-tuning objective: ranking good vs bad days is learnable, magnitudes are not
RMSE, R² | Regression error / fit | lower / higher | Diagnostics only; not comparable across baselines (§2.4)
Hit rate | Share of days with sign(prediction) = sign(target) | higher | Coarse directional accuracy
Sharpe | Unpenalised annualised Sharpe of the strategy | higher | Separates return quality from penalties
Vol ratio, penalties | std(s)/std(m); the two divisors above | vol ratio ≤ 1.2; penalties = 1 | Explains why a score is low
Ann. return, ann. volatility, max drawdown | Strategy profile | context | Risk profile

1.4 Validation protocol

The revised evaluation separates selection from scoring chronologically. Baselines have public-only comparisons with their original internal splitters. Proposed, improved and passive strategies additionally use the same four outer evaluation folds.

Public diagnostic (180 days). The pipeline is selected on preceding training rows and refitted before scoring this period. Because the block influenced historical development, its results cannot be treated as independent test evidence.

Nested purged temporal CV. For each outer fold, three inner temporal folds select model parameters, ensemble and allocation using only the outer training rows. Imputation is fitted inside each fit. Outer labels never control early stopping; iteration counts are selected internally and fixed before outer scoring. Purge=1 and the validation-head gap=20 are applied at both levels. This retrospective protocol removes within-run selection reuse but cannot erase earlier human exposure to the dataset.

2. Baselines

2.1 Choice of existing solutions

The competition ended before the project started, so we selected baselines from publicly available material:

The code of the top leaderboard solutions is not public. The 4th-place solution is available only as a write-up without code (baselines/04_4th_writeup.md), and it is explicitly not an ML solution (rule-based mean-reversion signal plus portfolio construction). We use it as a source of ideas, not as a baseline.

Many public notebooks report very high public-leaderboard scores (≈17). Because the public test set is a copy of the last 180 training rows, these scores come from fitting the answers and are not meaningful; the best private-leaderboard scores are much lower. Such notebooks were rejected.

The three source baselines represent a linear allocation model, a single LightGBM with temporal features, and an ElasticNet/XGBoost/LightGBM ensemble. B3 executes as a frozen ensemble in our replay; the online procedure advertised by the source does not run.

2.2 Adaptation protocol

The modelling logic of each notebook is kept as in the source. Changes are limited to data loading and result saving, and every change is marked in the notebook with an ADDED (PMLDL Stage 1) comment:

hard-coded /kaggle/input paths are replaced by the shared loader src.load_dataset();

the last 180 date_ids are removed from training (the original notebooks trained on them);

the Kaggle evaluation server is replaced by an offline replay that feeds the public block to the notebook's predict() one row at a time, rebuilding the lagged_* columns the API would provide;

scoring goes through src.metrics and results are written to results/leaderboard.csv.

Baselines retain their source preprocessing and internal validation. The B2 model and B3 hyperparameters can be restored from the submitted archive; replaying those artifacts reproduces their historical scores. New seeded searches are distinct experiments and are not guaranteed to recover parameters from the original unseeded searches. No baseline outer CV score is reported.

Analysis. All three baselines score below buy-and-hold. The dominant reason is the return penalty: on our data they are strongly under-invested (mean weight 0.12–0.45) during a rising market (buy-and-hold earned 23.8% annualised on this block), so their low volatility does not compensate for the missed return. B1 has a decent unpenalised Sharpe (1.39) but keeps only ~12% of capital in the market. B2 is the best baseline and is used as the base of our proposed model.

Why RMSE and hit rate are not compared across baselines. For B1–B3 the saved "prediction" is the allocation itself (B1 predicts an allocation target; B2 and B3 output positions), so RMSE against the excess-return target is meaningless (0.12–0.67) and the hit rate is distorted (B2's signal is often exactly 0). In B2 the quantile threshold of the discrete rule is computed on a single prediction during row-wise inference, so the rule effectively becomes 0/1 by sign; we kept this behaviour because it is part of the source code. Spearman IC and the Sharpe-family metrics are comparable across all models.

3. Proposed model



Figure 1. Replayed baseline allocations on the public diagnostic, with the passive w=1 reference. B3 is a frozen ensemble; no online refitting occurs.

3.1 Idea

The 4th-place write-up shows that short-horizon mean-reversion signals combined with volatility-aware weighting work without any learning, but its author names the fixed weighting as the limitation: reversion pays in choppy markets and loses in trends. A gradient-boosting model can, in principle, learn when such signals matter. The 61st-place pipeline was chosen as the learner because it already tunes on rank correlation rather than RMSE. The proposed model (proposed_model/proposed_hybrid_model.ipynb, name hybrid_domain_lgbm) is therefore a single LightGBM trained on the 61st-place temporal features plus a new block of domain features. The code is written from scratch in src/; only the class of indicators and the pipeline design are borrowed (attributed in the notebook header).

3.3 Model and training procedure

Model: LGBMRegressor (regression objective), target market_forward_excess_returns.

Nested model search: seeded Optuna TPE, 8 trials per family per selection on 3 inner purged temporal folds, at most 300 trees. The objective is mean inner Spearman IC. Budgets are fixed trial counts, with no wall-clock cutoff. These revised experiments differ from the original 120-trial search.

Each outer evaluation refits on its own earlier training rows using inner-selected parameters. The public diagnostic uses a fresh selection on the full development period followed by refitting.

Allocation: naive linear rule w = clip(1 + k · prediction, 0, 2) with a fixed k = 50. It stays centred on buy-and-hold and keeps the magnitude of the prediction, unlike B2's discrete rule. k was fixed, not tuned; selecting the allocation rule is deferred to the improvement stage.

Determinism: seed=42, LightGBM deterministic=True and force_col_wise=True; CPU thread budget=2. Versions and all selected parameters are in manifest.json.

4.4 Blend warm-up and historical development bug

The inverse-volatility blend requires history. The revised pipeline predicts the 60 chronologically preceding feature rows using the same fitted models, prepends these predictions for rolling weights, then scores only the evaluation rows. The models and imputation medians are fitted solely on the permitted earlier training rows. Warm-up rows do not enter validation objectives or scores; their labels are used only if already part of the permitted training set. Each inner/outer fold has its own history, including the feature rows in its purge/validation-head gap. Public inference follows the same warm-up procedure.

Historical context: an earlier version filled the undefined first 19 blend values with zero and reported 2.523 publicly; a later fix reported 1.239 with its historical models. Those numbers describe the earlier pipeline, not the revised nested experiment. The current code tests warm-up explicitly and does not assume that disjoint OOF folds are contiguous.

4.5 Development history and unsuccessful attempts

The final pipeline is the result of several iterations; we list the ones that changed conclusions.

First ensemble was worse than the proposed model. An early improved notebook (improved_ensemble) scored 0.011 on the public block, far below the proposed model. Contributing causes: model tuning against modified Sharpe under the binary rule (a coarse, noisy objective that overfits 4 folds) and a fixed allocation. This led to change 4 (IC for models, modified Sharpe for sizing).

Metric re-implementation. Our first modified_sharpe subtracted penalties instead of dividing by them and used arithmetic means; with that version the proposed model appeared to lose to B2. The function was rewritten to follow the official formula, and all notebooks were re-run.

Reproducibility of LightGBM. Re-running identical code gave different tuned parameters and different public scores, until deterministic=True, force_row_wise=True were added.

Allocation of the proposed model. During development the proposed model was also run with the binary rule, which scores higher on the public block (Table 15). The naive rule was kept in the final proposed model so that the allocation policy is fixed a priori and selected only in the improvement stage on CV data, not on the public block.

Separate attempt to improve the pipeline (a team member's branch: stabilised Ridge scaling and ensemble calibration). It did not improve the metrics and was not merged.

Blend warm-up bug (§4.4), found by an independent review of the improved notebook.

5.2 Analysis

Baselines versus proposed. The original baseline scores reproduce when their archived artifacts are replayed. They are public-only reference points with different preprocessing, not a controlled comparison on the nested outer folds. Across 20 paired fit seeds (0–19), the mean domain-block IC gain is -0.00167 (sample SD 0.01002), positive in 10 runs; paired t=-0.747, p=0.4642. Parameters are fixed within each outer fold. This measures fit-seed sensitivity, not 20 independent datasets or repeated searches. The original single-seed +0.013 does not establish a stable contribution.

Proposed vs. buy-and-hold. The proposed model is only marginally better than doing nothing (+0.04 on CV, −0.004 on the public block). A statistically weak but real ranking signal is not enough when the allocation rule is fixed.

Improved versus proposed. The selection procedure is now evaluated on later outer blocks. A gain is only descriptive unless the paired interval and temporal limitations support a stronger conclusion. The historical 70% tuning-fold increase is not independent evidence.

Failures and regime dependence are visible in the per-fold tables and wealth curves. A selected policy can lose to the passive reference on later blocks; the analysis retains those outcomes rather than switching the rule after looking at them.

5.3 Prediction quality

R² is close to zero for every model (CV mean 0.004–0.005, public −0.05 to 0.04) and the hit rate is 0.50–0.53. This is expected for daily excess returns and is the reason the models are tuned on rank correlation. The public-block IC (≈0.18) is more than twice the CV IC (≈0.075); with 180 observations the standard error of a correlation is about 0.075, so this difference is within noise and should not be read as better performance in recent years.

5.4 Statistical reliability

Paired moving-block bootstrap: 20-day non-circular blocks, 2000 resamples, seed 42, sampled within each fold; all strategies, market returns and risk-free returns use the same resampled rows. Results: cv: proposed − passive = +0.0321, 95% CI [-0.0157, +0.0864]; cv: improved − passive = +0.0774, 95% CI [-0.1757, +0.2706]; cv: improved − proposed = +0.0453, 95% CI [-0.1934, +0.2146]; public: proposed − passive = +0.0773, 95% CI [-0.0290, +0.3501]; public: improved − passive = +0.0645, 95% CI [-2.2721, +0.8594]; public: improved − proposed = -0.0128, 95% CI [-2.3388, +0.7039]. Intervals are conditional on fitted strategies and do not include retraining/selection uncertainty. Fit seeds share the same time series, so their paired t-test is a sensitivity diagnostic, not proof of market generalisation.

7. Conclusion

7.1 Summary of findings

Three public ML solutions were reproduced on one leak-safe split. On our data none of them beats buy-and-hold; the best (61st place LightGBM) reaches modified Sharpe 0.617 on the held-out block.

Domain features are used by the model, but their original single-seed +0.013 IC is insufficient evidence of a robust benefit. Across 20 paired fit seeds (0–19), the mean domain-block IC gain is -0.00167 (sample SD 0.01002), positive in 10 runs; paired t=-0.747, p=0.4642. Parameters are fixed within each outer fold. This measures fit-seed sensitivity, not 20 independent datasets or repeated searches. The original single-seed +0.013 does not establish a stable contribution.

The revised improved pipeline scores 0.4933 ± 0.4502 on outer CV versus 0.4159 ± 0.4511 for passive and 0.4480 ± 0.4347 for proposed. This replaces selection-fold improvement claims; the public results remain retrospective.

A silent warm-up bug had inflated an earlier public score from 1.24 to 2.52; it was found, fixed and kept as a regression check.

7.2 Limitations

Small diagnostic period. The public block has only 180 contiguous observations from one market regime and was consulted during development. Bootstrap precision cannot restore test independence.

Historical versus revised CV. The old model/blend/policy selection reused reported folds. The revised code nests all selection within outer training periods, with train-only imputation and no outer early stopping. Retrospective validation on a previously studied dataset still cannot establish prospective performance.

Selection and seed sensitivity. There are only four outer periods and a deliberately finite search budget. The 20 paired seeds hold hyperparameters fixed per fold and do not cover the variability of rerunning the entire search. Allocation choice may vary across periods.

Baselines are evaluated on the public block only and keep their original validation schemes (sklearn TimeSeriesSplit without purge).

Volatility and costs. Check the measured volatility ratios against 1.2 in the revised tables. The metric penalises excess volatility but does not enforce a hard constraint; transaction costs are not modelled and high turnover can reduce realised performance.

Metric. modified_sharpe is our re-implementation of the official formula; it was checked against the public description and code, but the organisers' evaluation service could not be used, and the forecasting-phase test data are unavailable.

No analysis of distribution shift between the training period and the public block was performed.

7.3 Future work

Prospective evaluation on newly arriving market data, plus more nested walk-forward windows and repeated full-search seeds. Nested temporal selection is already implemented in this revision.

Constrained sizing (calibrating the scale to a target volatility ratio ≤ 1.2 instead of tuning it on the score) and turnover smoothing.

Combining the model forecast with the no-learning reversion signal and shrinking the tilt towards w = 1.
